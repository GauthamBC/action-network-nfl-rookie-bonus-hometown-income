The first name called on draft night does not necessarily produce the biggest hometown payday. Cleveland native Arvell Reese, selected fifth, tops this ranking because his projected $31.8 million bonus equals a year of median household income for roughly 779 Cleveland households.

That puts him more than 158 households ahead of No. 1 pick Fernando Mendoza. The comparison shows how draft slot and local economics combine: two rookies can receive similarly extraordinary contracts, but those paydays carry very different weight in the places that shaped them.

How we built the ranking

Every decision was made to keep the 32-player comparison consistent, transparent and reproducible.

STEP 01

Defined the field

The study includes all 32 first-round selections in the 2026 NFL Draft. Second-round players were excluded to preserve a complete, clearly defined group.

STEP 02

Verified each hometown

We used the hometown listed by a player's final college athletics roster or official college announcement, not birthplace, college location or academy location.

STEP 03

Matched the income geography

U.S. figures use 2024 ACS five-year Table B19013. County data is used when a hometown lacks a suitable place-level match. Ili'ili uses the 2020 Island Areas Census; Ottawa uses Statistics Canada's 2021 Census profile.

STEP 04

Calculated the multiple

Projected signing bonus ÷ median annual household income. Ottawa's comparison converts the bonus to Canadian dollars using the Bank of Canada's July 24, 2026 USD/CAD rate of 1.4093.
